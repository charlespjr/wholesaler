# Agent Call Q&A Playbook — for every VA deal sheet / agent call-back

> Standing reference. When building a VA deal sheet or agent call-back, **always
> include this Q&A** (tailored to the specific deal's numbers). This is what a
> listing agent will probe a cash buyer on, and how Paragon's VA answers it.
> Voice: human, confident, plain — never robotic.

## The #1 question: "Are you the buyer, or are you wholesaling / assigning this?"
Answer it **straight** — don't dodge, don't lie. Wholesaling/assigning is legal and
agents deal with it daily. What they actually fear is a flaky buyer who ties up the
listing and disappears. So be honest, then pivot hard to **performance**:

> "I'm a cash buyer. I buy through my company, Paragon, and it's written 'and/or
> assigns,' so I may bring in one of my funding partners to close — but either way
> you get a real close: earnest money up front, proof of funds, and we perform.
> You're not chasing me."

Three rules for the VA on this:
1. **Never deny it's assignable** — the contract literally says "and/or assigns."
   Getting caught lying kills the deal and our name.
2. **Anchor on performance** — earnest money + proof of funds + we close. That's what
   wins agents.
3. **Keep the inspection period tight (10 days)** — a long due-diligence window is what
   makes agents suspect we're just shopping it. Short DD = serious buyer.

**Caveat:** on **REO / bank-owned** listings, assignment is usually prohibited — there
we drop "and/or assigns" and **double-close** instead (doubleclose.com). On a regular
seller listing, straight assignment is fine.

## The other questions an agent will ask (and the answers)

| Agent asks | VA says |
|---|---|
| "Can you send proof of funds?" | "Yes — I'll include POF with the written terms." (POF via doubleclose.com.) |
| "How much earnest money — is it hard?" | "$1,000 to title within 3 business days. Happy to make it firm after our inspection period." |
| "How fast can you close?" | "About 3 weeks, or on the seller's timeline." |
| "Cash, no financing?" | "All cash — no loan, no appraisal contingency." |
| "How'd you get to that number?" | Walk the rehab math: worth ~$ARV fixed, needs ~$repairs of work, builder pays ~$builder_max after, so we land at our offer. "It's not a lowball — it's what a full rehab allows." |
| "Do you want to see it / walk it?" | "We buy off photos and disclosures, then verify in our 10-day inspection." (NO walkthrough — hard rule.) |
| "Are you actually going to close, or tie it up and bail?" | "We put up earnest money and proof of funds up front — we perform. I'm not going to tie up your listing and disappear." |
| "What's your inspection / due-diligence period?" | "10 days — short on purpose, so you know we're serious." |
| "Who's your title/closing company?" | "We close through title; I'll get you the company with the written terms." |
| "Can you close in your name?" | "Yes — we close in our company name, Paragon Government Solutions LLC." |

## Hard stops the VA must never break
- Never offer above the deal's stated **ceiling/MAO**. Higher = stop, call Charles.
- Never promise to visit, tour, or walk the property.
- Never send written terms before getting the agent's email.
- Never send a signed offer without Charles okaying it first.
- Never deny we may assign — be honest, anchor on performance.

## Why honesty wins here
Agents aren't scared of wholesalers — they're scared of buyers who don't perform.
Lead with earnest money, proof of funds, a fast clean close, and a short inspection
window, and the "are you wholesaling" question stops mattering.
